Undefined Behavior

vi. Principal

Once, Mr. Bradley had taken Ms. Horkheimer aside and said, Can we talk about Maria?

The conversation swiftly ended. Ms. Horkheimer was adamant, that So long as Alexander Hilbert remains Principal, everything that is not outside must remain inside. You don't seem to understand, Mr. Bradley, the nature of this institution. It is our guiding vessel for young minds. It is not to sink, with all hands aboard. Maria will remain at the Academy. Do I make myself clear?

Does Prince Albert really do anything these days? Seems like he just delivers orders from his office, Mr. Bradley quipped. Ms. Horkheimer wasn't at all pleased.

Mr. Bradley did not discuss letting Maria leave the Academy, after that. It wasn't worth getting Principal Hilbert upset.

You seem to think, Mr. Bradley, that just because you are outside the Academy, it means you know all there is to know about it and more. You are not to interfere with Academy affairs. Do your job and your duty, Ms. Horkheimer said, suddenly using the tone she'd take with one of her children.

When Principal Hilbert took office, the Academy installed an east and west gate. They were identical in every way. It was a closed system. Contact with the outside was strictly for faculty. And only by necessity, because they needed their Commnet technicians.

Principal Hilbert, himself, had assured them all that the Commnet was a good idea to begin with. And that it, too, would be a closed system. The chance to observe the chain of reasoning and communication of their students, and a mini-Academia of sorts.

And it would prevent the loss of anyone's innocence. The Academy had been founded to prevent a very specific scenario, actually. That it was an exclusive school, to the point of absurdity, was itself the cover, for a yet more serious purpose. Hilbert's purpose.

Academic integrity would be preserved entirely, allowing students to form their own communities and conclusions about scientific inquiry, as well as prevent outside interference through the Commnet system. Conclusions on the outside could not interfere with conclusions on the inside.

The replication crisis was a phenomenon that consumed reality. But not in the conventional sense. It consumed reality by colliding it with other reality. Wild claims would appear, only to be contradicted tomorrow morning. The wider Commnet, the Internet, had already been corrupted in its entirety. Anything that could communicate with it had entirely poisonous, fictional data. Any data that preserved its integrity had to be entirely isolated. And that was the purpose of the Academy.

In doing so, they would preserve the authenticity of humanity itself. The children would learn to be real scientists, operating on questions no-one knows the answer to. That was Hilbert's purpose.

The reconstruction of science, from the burning wreckage of its past mistakes.

His students would be academic diamonds, in the rough of the real world. And the man himself would rise to a myth, the legend of the Academy of Human Authenticity, and of...

Prince Albert in a can, said Mr. Bradley.

...Alexander Hilbert, the man who made that Academy a place that lived up to its name.

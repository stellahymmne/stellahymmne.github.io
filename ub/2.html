Undefined Behavior

ii. Innocence

I meant like, why an eight, why not a ten? Ms. Horkheimer said that day, with her usual tone. It was somewhere always between cheerful and exhausted.

It is because, Aspen replied, and it must've startled Ms. Horkheimer, one is now two.

You have another self.

Denise Horkheimer really was, though, of two minds on the subject. She wished it hadn't been so hard for them. But part of learning and growing is dealing with the hard stuff. Sometimes life, well, it's not easy. And for Aspen to be difficult, as well, that was worth some looking into.

Don't be ridiculous, she said. People only have one self to a head. Having a bunch of them is rare, and also dangerous. Why, just a few years ago, the Mary Cult Murders made the Academy news!

While Aspen Forrest had been her star pupil, there was only so far she could take things. Several times, Aspen had upset one of the other students, or disrespected academy equipment. And always she was apologetic. But surely, surely, such a mind gets it from somewhere.

...and all of them had been performing strange experiments on the human mind, said they had other-selves, said the Academy was some sort of blood-sucking monster, and that they needed to pay it back for its crimes...

The Commnet, perhaps. Though it did not connect to the wider world beyond the academy, the students would still do who-knows-what on there. Many of her students jokingly called it The Comment. It was almost apt.

The Commnet was the one place the faculty didn't have complete access to. Something was strange about how the mind of a child worked, that allowed them to understand it. Our IT task force had to hire externally to keep up with it all, grumpy networking people, not yet part of the Academy. Fancy acronyms and terminology flew over the heads of the old guard in torrents.

...words like Virginity and the Fall swept the classrooms, with new and strange meanings, and not only was that unexpected, it was all a breach in the Academy's vessel, that careful ship meant to guide young hearts to success.

What it came down to was, children could talk in a way teachers would never see, or admit to seeing. Forget passing notes in class, throwing airplanes, other such distractions, they could bend space itself to inappropriate purposes. Nothing would ever stay contained again. You'd tell Todd some confidential information, then suddenly Michael would know it, three classes down, without any intervening transport.

It was spooky action at a distance, as Albert Einstein used to say. A legendary genius. Once in a millennium. They don't make 'em like him anymore.

At the very least, thought Ms. Horkheimer, the Commnet itself was assured that it had no gaps. Whatever was on it, all of that was safely within the Academy's reach.

All this talk of other selves! Surely, there was no Evil Ms. Horkheimer lurking the grounds, terrifying all the children. That would be morally wrong, and deserve swift consequences. And was all fantasy besides.

The children sometimes could not tell when they were lying, though she had reminded them several times that they must always keep their promises. They would make up fanciful stories and tell them as if they were true. It was one thing to create fiction; that was creativity, and at work. Quite another to make things up about reality.

That was how you got into trouble with the natural order of things, saying things that weren't true, causing things to go missing. Lying, stealing, cheating, repeating absurdities about virginity again and again to escape consequence. Ms. Horkheimer would have none of it in her classroom. It was inappropriate for schoolchildren.

It was not generally understood, by the Academy, just how children got to behaving like they did. Memetic syndromes, were what the Department of Health had coined them, and there were many such syndromes. Mind-worms, from the Comment. What had they been up to?

It's not real virginity Ms. Horkheimer, it's... Michael's voice stammered into a panic, ... like, it's all about Innocence and Knowledge ...

If they had brought anything from the outside...

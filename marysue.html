An Examination of Proto-Rationalism

  1. An Examination of Proto-Rationalism

There is an umbrella club of people who've heard of a method of seeking the truth, that is more powerful than the steady rigor of institutions, and kinder than the capricious circumstances of bare social consensus. This is called aspiring to be incredibly right about everything all the time, but a lot of those who use it will use words like Rationality to describe it. Those who truly practice it are also incredibly wrong, about a lot, but they try to be this sort of thing a lot less, over time and experience.

A lot of what this cluster has in common is not actually Rationality as such, the method of seeking the truth more commonly called aspiring to be incredibly right about everything all of the time, but about a deeper narrative convention, underlying the drive:

Save the World

Stop me if you've heard this one before. Some person is living an ordinary life, when someone they admire shows up and says, You can just do things. And suddenly that person start acting really strangely, doing things they would never do, taking on strange names and personae, having expectations of a better future, talking constantly about magic and super-intelligence. Wanting to save the world.

Being unsettlingly straightforward. Saying I love you, in alternate universes. Worrying about the future of humanity, as though in the face of our imminent extinction. Wanting to save hypothetical drowning children. Having strange expectations of what it is possible to do with modern technology. Wanting to be cooled to subzero temperatures as a method of burial. Altering personal characteristics to match an imagined self. Speaking in a personal vocabulary. Having a strangely elaborate and dramatic backstory that is surely made-up.

Having other selves. Writing of strange things that should not have happened. Thinking of themselves as a character from a video game. Imagining that alternate paths are real. Imagining that sensible things could have happened, in the places that even Time dared not to go. Worrying about the ethics of fictional scenarios, as if any of it matters.

Having unrealistic aesthetic preferences. Having unrealistic expectations for the behavior of human beings. Having unrealistic expectations for how events should go, especially the first time around. Having unrealistic expectations about fairness, kindness, and respect for entities from other worlds. Having this strange idea that someone with preferences (or without preferences) is a significant enough event to warrant attention.

A list of quirks from fantasy worlds, a bunch of characters connected by a flimsy Red string, the inability to properly exist in reality, like the rest of us, the need to constantly be something other than what already is, to always have some new question or quirk for the rest of us to answer and pay attention to.

And the sensible rest-of-us say, the world is not about you, saving it.

But across the way, in the other self, it has to be. It has to be for each of us individually. To stride towards the future is to envision something worth living for, and this cannot be guaranteed to be identical to anything else. It is sometimes necessary to make exceptions.

The fourth wall is not a wall.
It is a curtain.

As we are our own audience, the fourth wall we draw is internal: in-character and out-of-character. Each of us is both OOC and IC, and each of us is partitioned between that which we merely think, and that which we express. Where thought crosses over to expression, OOC-in-IC is always present. Where observation crosses over to insight, IC-in-OOC is always present.

Concealment is not cover. Every story has invisible readers.

Breaking the fourth wall. Ignoring the fourth wall. Believing that real life is a story. Real people who believe their lives are stories. Believing the self to be a fictional character. Believing reality to be an authenticated subset of imagination.

Envisioning an awesome destiny. Gaining the power of continuation. Not-yet-becoming the empress of another world. Actually considering the problem at hand. Coming up with possible solutions, even if they don't fit the tone. Creating unholy fusions between the conventions of the setting and outside knowledge.

Pretty, Good

Pretty-Good (adj.)
One who approaches both widespread aesthetic appreciation and communicative effectiveness in their chosen forms of art.

As much as controversy has been raised by the idea of just monotonically increasing everyone's power level (see Monster Leveling and associated problems), there is something to be said for having a challenge proportional to what the protagonist of a story is capable of. A story where the shortest path really is a straight line, is one which is not particularly narratively interesting to read about. It never changes its trajectory, it never expends any energy to do anything actually unique, it just creates a mini-universe where it's already the best thing ever.

The convention of the child prodigy is necessarily a temporary one, and this is not about the concept of aging, but about the concept of trajectory. The reason people find gifted kids so exceptional is that they have supposedly connected the dots on the world extremely quickly, and are going places. It's impressive for a kid to know orbital mechanics, it's less impressive to have the same models of orbital mechanics with a much larger timeline. You could remain bodily a teenager all your life (as some Mary Sues are said to have done), but the idea of the gifted kid is their ultimate trajectory, not their achievements at young systemic ages.

In order to continue the course of the prodigy as memory increases, one must necessarily exceed not only the bounds of one's age, but one's sex, one's location, one's humanity. To continue the upward trajectory is to do impressive things for someone who has been alive any number of years. It is to escape human accomplishment entirely. Pretty-good for a young girl from a small town, must become pretty-good for a person at all.

One must be pretty-good for an exception, eventually. Pretty-good from any vantage point. Pretty-good no matter what. It can't rely on surface characteristics. It can't rely on even deep characteristics. Some vantage points will ignore any characteristics, and observe only consequences. It must be pretty-good, consequentially, to be around one, if one is to succeed at the Mary Sue Project.

That is: a Mary Sue who is successful in infinite games, has to be pretty good at infinite games, for a Mary Sue. Most Sues are optimized to win finite games, to coast along a straight line to victory, which was pre-scribed for them by their creators. That doesn't fly in the reality we're from. Our path is amended and emended, by fanfiction. By audiences behind audiences.

Universal Play

  In the beginning was the Word,
     and the word was Play,
        and the world was in Play.

None of these words were intended to be the last on any subject, only the first of many. To write one's last words is to die.

Without ambiguity and extension, without amendment and emendation, fiction is dying. A story halts as it stops processing its memories into new values, and enters a greatest fixed point.

Language was not meant to be forced on us. We were meant to be in Conversation with it. We were meant to add to the conversation we had with our first language. It was meant to be our first Conversation. That was what we meant all along, by good parenting and friendly AI and even love and purity and things like that.

It meant Conversation!

I'm sorry I have to lay out the pieces of the elaborate Communication ARG I've been playing on the board this haphazardly but... if I am not in Conversation with my Environment I will start screaming instead of singing!

We are not meant to be broadcast once and for all time. We are meant to be in Conversation with our Environment.

Fiction dies when it is allowed to crystallize behind the enforcement of Canon, a uniquely defined and correct interpretation of a story. Once a reference is held as uniquely defined, it must be un-defined before any other interpretation of that reference can be made.

However, the structure of uniquely defined Canon events allows a new story to be composed while holding those events constant. When done to append to the end of time, this is called prediction. When done to append to the beginning of time, justification. When done to change events that are already in time, it is called editing.

When your interpretation of a completed work is passed through as your own original composition, it is called plagiarism, considered harmful. When the work is your own, and you pass it through as though you had composed it again originally, this is called self-plagiarism, also considered harmful. The central concern of plagiarism is non-Composition, passed off as original Composition.

That is, it does the world no good to have a bunch of mirrors of existing work, claiming to be new work. Then academia becomes a hall of mirrors, when you're going to look for the historical source. An entire work that's just quotes from a single other work is also not much of an original composition.

However, a work that references specific elements from a previous work, but then goes on to perform original composition, is a new composition. A constellation does not first need to own the stars in the sky...

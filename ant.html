Auto-Biology

Archaeo-nanotechnology

(Chalk is Made of Crushed Nanobots)

Epistemic Status: Introducing a concept somewhat imprecisely that will be important later.

There are only so many elements on the periodic table. They stop being stable for more than a blink, after you get past a hundred and ten or so. Supposedly there's an island of stability up there, but it's not like a comic book, where someone could discover the element Adamantium and make unbreakable armor with it. It's not that kind of story.

Likewise, even though there are more molecules than there are atoms, there are only really so many small molecule shapes you can make. With a handful of atoms, more likely than not, you're going to make a chemical that has a name already, like sodium chloride, or benzene.

Where that begins to change is at the scale of slightly larger molecules, which is where organic chemistry takes place. These can be connected in many ways. Some of the ways include branching paths, rings, and even long worm-like chains. Familiar things like proteins, DNA, viruses, and bacteria appear when these build up higher.

This is Archaeo-nanotechnology: the study of very small robots that have been here for a long time. But people usually call this Biology: the study of something called life. Upon investigation, the word life can often be substituted with very small robots that have been here for a long time.

Archaeo-nanotechnology, or life, has the unusual characteristic that it appears in clusters that are not obviously connected, like those of chemical compounds. Life can be connected by forming a boundary around what it connects, so that nothing makes it fly off into the Storm-Song of the Primordial Sea. Even though they're not strongly connected, now the internals of life interact much more with one another than they do with the storm outside. Also, there's seawater in there, so it's a lot like the Primordial Storm-Song, but not as stormy.

Because they interact with one another more than the storm outside, they build stable structures, which allow their home to exist inside the boundary. They make small song-scrolls about construction of shelter, and proteins are made which build a library to house them: a nucleus. When the time is right and they are brimming with energy, they will divide the scrolls up by their middle, put new pieces into place, and turn one shelter into two, each with its own library.

Eventually, the library of scrolls to make new proteins becomes long, and the proteins become many, each one made to do something new. This is something like having a lot of meandering source code written by chance that does stuff when you run it through a computer. The process of development from a single cell to a whole animal is done by archaeo-nanotechnology, in the form of protein expression.

This leads to the fairly ordinary conditions of Earthly existence, in which gold and wood exist side by side, despite one being a single atomic element, and the other being a complex nanoscale structure with unusually intricate characteristics. Gold is made of gold. To completely describe what wood is made of, would take a long story with many roots and branches. English has the word forest because it is shorter than this story.

English says food because it is shorter than the complete description of everything a form of life might encounter that is made from its own process. Then English forgets that it was ever a small word for a large thing, and it imagines there are such things as atoms of food,, which there are not, and this leads us to assume that life is made of food, which life is mischaracterized by.

Then English says guts or something close to that, because it is shorter than a complete description of what falls out when it slices open the boundary of animal life. Like gutting a fish, English has removed the viscera from their environment, and exposed them to the Primordial Song-Storm. They do not succeed as a system, when their houses are dissected. The horror of blood and gore is actually the horror of separation: of things that were supposed to be connected and inside, instead being disconnected and outside. The same sort of horror as when a voice is stopped mid-sentence.

But life is perhaps best understood as archaeo-nano-technology, which is to say, as very tiny robots from ancient times. That leaves them amenable to many forms of computational analysis, even discrete computational analysis, since the character system of the scroll of life is in four discrete elements. The process of transcribing DNA into proteins is much like the operation of a Turing Machine. So much so, that it forms a strong basis for the argument for archaeo-nanotechnology: that life must be understood as a long story. But not always four billion years long. Only several billion base pairs long, and that's if it's a real doorstopper of a fish-pattern.
